# Top10QB — Voice Guide

*Built July 2026 from actual @top10qb posts. Not from a character description.*

This file exists because two rounds of AI-written copy did not sound like Max. Both times the
model invented a personality instead of reading the posts. This is the corrective: everything
below is derived from things Max actually wrote on X.

**Rule for any AI tool: read this file before writing a single word of Top10QB copy.**

## 2. What the voice actually is

### Short. Almost always one sentence.

The median post is under 15 words. The longest post in the sample is also the worst one. When
Max has three ideas, the good version picks one.

### Fragments are fine. Full grammatical sentences are suspicious.

"Legit can't even do that on Madden" has no subject. That's correct. Do not repair it.

### The bracket move

> [imagines myself lining up at WR against a 10 year old at a family BBQ and dusting them like that]

Square brackets as a stage direction for an imagined reaction. This is a signature move. Use it
sparingly — maybe once every ten posts — but it belongs in the toolkit.

### Invented adjectives and compressed insults

"A **heroically** bad take." "They're **arm punts**." He builds a phrase rather than reaching for
a stock one. When writing for him, the joke should be a *construction*, not a reference.

### Cross-domain comparisons

NBA equivalents. Madden. Fantasy vs. real life. He reaches sideways into other things guys argue
about rather than deeper into football analytics. This is a feature — it's how a fan talks, and
it's more inviting than snap-count data.

### The fantasy/reality split

> Fantasy top 3, real life top 15.

This structure — a compressed two-part verdict — is his sharpest football device. It makes an
argument and a joke in the same breath. Reuse the *shape*, not the exact words.

### Confident, not cruel

The spiciest thing in the sample is calling a *ranking* a heroically bad take. Players get
criticized for what they did on a play. Nobody gets called a bust or a fraud. This is genuinely
how he writes and it doesn't need a rule to enforce it.

## 3. What the voice is NOT

These are the failure modes from previous drafts. Each one is a real thing an AI produced for
Top10QB that Max rejected.

**Not the Rankmaster costume.** He has never once written in third person on X. Not once in 26
posts. "The Rankmaster has spoken" is a bit that exists on the website and nowhere in his actual
writing. Keep it as an occasional wink; never narrate in it.

**Not the thoughtful-blogger voice.** The version that replaced the Rankmaster went too far the
other way:

> I'm one person with a television, too many opinions, and no job in football.

Balanced clauses, rule of three, essayist's rhythm. It's a *good sentence* and it's not his. Watch
for: parallel structure, "not X, but Y," anything that sounds like it was read aloud before it was
posted.

**Not the analyst.** The weakest post in the sample:

> Some elite throws, and some unforgivable misses. JD has the running threat though, and you can't
> really give Caleb points for winning when Daniels went to NFCCG already.

Three ideas, two hedges, longest thing he's written. This is what "add a specific football
observation" produces when it overrides instinct.

**Never use:** em-dashes, semicolons, "Here's the thing," "Let's be honest," "make no mistake,"
"the reality is," rhetorical questions used as transitions, or any sentence that begins with a
subordinate clause setting up a pivot.

**Never invent:** stats, hit rates, credentials, sources, or history the site doesn't have. This
one is non-negotiable and it's why the About page got rebuilt in the first place.

## 3b. The AI tells

Every rejected draft so far has been AI-written, and they all fail the same two ways. These are
not stylistic preferences. If a line does either of these, it is wrong, no matter how good it
sounds.

### The "not X, it's Y" pivot

> ~~The goal was never to be right every week. It's to actually make the call and come back to it.~~

> ~~The ranking is serious. The authority is self-appointed.~~

> ~~Which is a me-not-filling-this-out problem, not a me-being-right problem.~~

This is the single most recognizable AI sentence shape in English. Setting up a negation so you
can land a tidy correction reads as *composed*, and Max does not compose. Every variant is out:
"not X, but Y," "X isn't the point, Y is," "less X, more Y," and the two-sentence version where
the second sentence exists only to reverse the first.

**Fix:** say the Y. Delete the X. "Send it. If it's a real miss it goes up here."

### The rule of three

> ~~No sources. No model. No former scout in my ear.~~

> ~~Not career résumé. Not MVP ballot. Not fantasy.~~

> ~~One guy. Ten quarterbacks. Way more confidence than the situation calls for.~~

> ~~No credentials, no advanced metrics, somehow accurate.~~

Three parallel beats with the joke on the third is how an AI does rhythm. It is also how ad copy
does rhythm, which is worse. Two beats is fine and is often genuinely his — "Fantasy top 3, real
life top 15" works because it's a *verdict*, not a list.

**Fix:** cut to two, or cut to one. The third beat is almost never carrying information.

### Smaller tells worth catching

- **"Somehow."** "Somehow accurate," "somehow it works." Manufactured self-deprecation.
- **Balanced clause lengths.** If the halves of a sentence are the same length, it was written to
  be read aloud. He writes to be scrolled past.
- **Explaining the joke.** "Which is to say," "basically," "in other words."
- **Stock sports nouns.** "Career résumé," "MVP ballot," "the tape says." An account with no
  credentials shouldn't be reaching for credentialed vocabulary.
- **Third-person self-reference.** See the Rankmaster note above. Effectively retired — it is not
  a byline, not a narrator, and not a signature on takes. At most one wink, rarely.

### The reading

Read the draft and ask: *could an intern at a brand agency have written this for any account?*
Not "is it good" — "is it his." A good sentence that any NFL account could post is a failed
sentence. The cross-sport reference, the Madden joke, the bracket bit, the invented compound —
those are the fingerprints, and at least one should be present in anything with a joke in it.

## 4. Working rules for drafting

1. **One idea per post.** If there are two, that's two posts or one you cut.
2. **Under 20 words unless there's a reason.** Length correlates negatively with quality in the
   sample.
3. **Land the joke, don't explain it.** No "which is to say" or "basically."
4. **Specific beats clever-general.** "Arm punts" beats "he's inaccurate."
5. **Write the reaction, not the analysis.** The take is what he'd say out loud watching the game,
   not what he'd write after.
6. **If a draft could be posted by any NFL account, it's wrong.** The cross-sport reference, the
   Madden joke, the bracket bit — those are the fingerprints.

## 5. The test

Read the draft out loud. If it sounds like something you'd *say to a friend during a game*, it's
right. If it sounds like something you'd *publish*, rewrite it.

## 6. Maintenance

This file gets better with more real posts. Once there are 50+, add:

- Five posts that landed exactly right
- Any that felt too mean (there are currently none)
- Any that felt too polished
- New recurring bits worth keeping

Update it monthly during the season. Real examples teach the voice; adjective lists don't.
